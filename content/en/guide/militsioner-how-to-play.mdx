## Escape Routes and Beginner Tips

There is no single exit from the town. The developer's own description lists stealing, selling, trading, breaking in, and sneaking out as legitimate approaches — plus seduction as a fallback when brute force fails.

| Approach | Risk Level | What It Rewards | Best Used When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sneak out | Low | A clean, quiet escape | The Policeman is distracted or calm |
| Steal and sell | Medium | Cash for tools and bribes | You've mapped item locations |
| Take a job | Low | Legitimate access and stable income | You want a route that keeps NPCs unharmed |
| Break in / sabotage | High | Fast shortcuts | You accept long-term consequences |
| Seduce or befriend | Variable | Unique outcomes and mood swings | You've banked goodwill items like flowers |

Here are practical tips that carry across every attempt:

- **Scout before you commit.** Walk the perimeter of the town, note guard sightlines, and identify which buildings you can enter without a plan.
- **Bank consumables.** Flowers and tradeable trinkets are currency, leverage, and gifts all at once. Never sell your last one.
- **Chain small crimes.** A stolen item funds a bought tool, which opens a door, which reveals a route.
- **Fail forward.** Getting caught is not a game over — it's data. The next attempt starts with more information.
- **Keep plans modular.** Build an escape out of three or four independent steps so one failure doesn't collapse everything.
- **Test the Policeman's patience.** Try a small provocation in a low-stakes area and watch how his behavior changes.

If you're stuck, switch categories entirely rather than grinding the same route. The game is nonlinear by design, and the same wall usually has a door somewhere else.

## Managing the Colossal Policeman's Mood — and Living With the Consequences

The Colossal Policeman isn't a static obstacle; he's a character with moods, and the store description explicitly invites you to anger, flatter, disappoint, deceive, or even romance him. That means your social choices are as mechanical as your stealth choices.

| Your Action | Likely Effect | Smart Follow-Up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give flowers or flirt | Warms him up; softer responses | Try a favor, a date, or a bold request |
| Insult or openly defy | Raises hostility | Lay low and rebuild goodwill |
| Deceive him | Short-term openings, long-term risk | Use the window immediately |
| Stay neutral and productive | Keeps options open | Scout and bank resources |

Consequences are permanent within a run. The developer gives a concrete example: if you conspire with the train driver to sneak aboard and the plan collapses, the train is wrecked and the driver is injured. Take a job at the station instead, and the driver stays fine. The town remembers, so a "clever" shortcut can quietly delete a future ally.

Replay is built around this memory. When the Policeman catches you, the next attempt isn't a reset — he recalls what you did last time and adjusts. That's why varied approaches beat repetition, and why Militsioner how to play ultimately comes down to reading the simulation rather than memorizing a walkthrough.
