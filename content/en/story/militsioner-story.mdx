## The Militsioner Story Begins With a Crime You Didn't Commit

What if you were arrested for something you never did — in a town whose rules seem to rewrite themselves overnight? That's the hook of the **Militsioner story**, a Kafkaesque immersive sim from developer TALLBOYS and publisher CRITICAL REFLEX. It matters because this narrative isn't wallpaper: it's the machine that powers every escape attempt, every mood swing from the Colossal Policeman, and every consequence the town quietly files away for later.

You don't play a hero. You play an ordinary nobody with no achievements worth mentioning, which is exactly what makes the arrest so absurd. No interrogation scene, no evidence, no explanation — just a surreal town, a passport, and a half-eaten banana to your name. From that moment, the entire Militsioner story becomes a negotiation between your imagination and one very large, very watchful obstacle.

Here's the premise in its simplest form:

| Story element | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| Your role | An unremarkable citizen with no notable past |
| The inciting incident | Sudden arrest for a crime you can't recall committing |
| Starting inventory | A passport and a half-eaten banana |
| The setting | A surreal town you are not allowed to leave |
| The central obstacle | A Colossal Policeman who tracks your every move |
| The core loop | Plan an escape, succeed or get caught, then try again smarter |

Notice what's missing: there's no mission briefing, no objective marker explaining the "correct" way out. The game hands you a problem, not a solution. That's the whole point.

## The Colossal Policeman Is the Beating Heart of the Militsioner Story

Strip away the town, the trains, and the flowers, and the Militsioner story still stands on one relationship: you and HIM. The Colossal Policeman exists to keep you inside the town's borders. He is enormous, he is always nearby, and he notices things — including the things you'd rather he didn't.

But here's where the narrative gets interesting. He isn't a scripted wall. He has moods, and those moods are gameplay systems. The official material openly invites you to anger, flatter, disappoint, deceive, or even romance him. He's been described as moody lately, which is a strange thing to say about a giant lawman and also a very useful thing to know.

| Emotional lever | How it plays out |
| --- | --- |
| Anger | Provoke him and he becomes a harder, more attentive obstacle |
| Flattery | Soften his attitude to buy yourself breathing room |
| Disappointment | Disappoint him and he may change how closely he watches you |
| Deception | Mislead him about your intentions or your movements |
| Romance | Court him with gifts — flowers work — and possibly take him on a date |

That last row is not a joke tucked into a press release. Seduction is presented as a genuine alternative when brute force and theft both fail. A game that lets you flirt your way past a kaiju-sized police officer is making a statement about what kind of story it wants to tell: one where the "correct" solution is whatever you can justify.

## The Town Remembers: Consequences in the Militsioner Story

Many stealth games reset the world when you fail. The Militsioner story doesn't. It keeps receipts.

The clearest example from the official material involves the train. You conspire with the driver to sneak aboard. The plan collapses. The train is wrecked and the driver is injured — and that injury is on you. Take a different route next time, get a job at the train station instead of plotting with its employee, and the driver walks away fine. Same town, same goal, completely different collateral damage.

That design philosophy shapes how you should approach the Militsioner story:

- **Assume every action is logged.** The town tracks what you do and say, not just whether you got caught.
- **Treat NPCs as long-term investments.** Someone you exploit in run one may be an asset — or a roadblock — in run two.
- **Think in chains, not steps.** Breaking in somewhere might fund a trade that unlocks a route that avoids the train entirely.
- **Reconsider "victimless" crimes.** There are usually victims. They just aren't you.

| Your approach | Ripple effect on the town |
| --- | --- |
| Conspire with the train driver | Train destroyed, driver injured, route closed |
| Take a job at the train station | Driver stays healthy, route remains viable |
| Steal and sell goods | Money and inventory change, suspicion rises |
| Trade honestly | Slower progress, fewer enemies |
| Seduce the Policeman | Relationship shifts, watchfulness may drop |

The closing question the developers pose is telling: you started out believing you were innocent — will you still be innocent by the time you get out? That's the emotional spine of the Militsioner story. Escape is the goal, but the cost is measured in who you became along the way.

## Escape Routes: How the Militsioner Story Branches

Because Militsioner is built as a nonlinear immersive sim, freedom isn't a single door. You explore the town, scout the map, and assemble an exit from whatever the environment gives you. Stealing, selling, trading, breaking in, and sneaking out are all on the table.

| Escape approach | What it demands | Risk profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stealth and sneaking | Patience, route knowledge, low profile | Low noise, high tension |
| Theft | Timing, nerve, a fence for the goods | Raises suspicion fast |
| Trading and selling | Found resources, like flowers | Slow but safe |
| Breaking and entering | Tools, opportunity, escape plan | High reward, high heat |
| Employment | Taking a legitimate job for access | Quiet, time-consuming |
| Seduction | Gifts, flattery, repeated contact with HIM | Unpredictable but flexible |
| Direct confrontation | Brute force | Usually the worst option |

A useful way to think about it: each route is a different genre. Stealth is a puzzle game, theft is a heist, trading is an economy sim, and the romance path is — genuinely — a character drama with a giant policeman. The Militsioner story lets you pick which one you're playing.

If you want the full official breakdown of these systems, the [Militsioner page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1373530/Militsioner/) lays out the mechanics alongside the demo announcement.

## Every Escape Attempt Rewrites the Militsioner Story

Getting caught isn't a game over screen. It's a story beat.

When the Colossal Policeman stops you, you don't restart from zero — you restart from *informed*. He remembers what you did last time and adjusts his behavior accordingly. That means your second attempt isn't a retry; it's a sequel with a smarter antagonist.

| | First attempt | Second attempt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your knowledge | Guessing at routes | Knows which paths are watched |
| The Policeman's behavior | Baseline patrol logic | Adapted to your last method |
| Town state | Clean slate | Carries consequences from run one |
| Best strategy | Experiment broadly | Exploit a specific blind spot |

This is why replay value sits prominently among the game's tags. The Militsioner story is designed to be told multiple times, and each telling changes because you changed first.

## FAQ

**What is the Militsioner story actually about?**
You're an ordinary person arrested in a surreal town for a crime you didn't commit. A Colossal Policeman prevents you from leaving, and your job is to find a way out — through stealth, theft, trade, deception, or even romance. The Militsioner story is really about how far you'll bend your own innocence to escape.

**Can you really romance the Colossal Policeman?**
Yes, according to the official material. Flowers can be kept as a backup gift to flirt with him, and the game explicitly mentions the possibility of taking HIM on a date. It's positioned as an alternative when force and theft fail.

**Is Militsioner out yet?**
No. The planned release date is 2027, but a free demo became available on October 8, 2026, and a playtest is open for access requests. You can wishlist it on Steam to be notified when it launches.

**Do your choices carry between escape attempts?**
Yes. The town remembers what you do and say, and the Colossal Policeman recalls your previous method and adjusts his behavior. That's why the Militsioner story is meant to be replayed with new approaches rather than brute-forced the same way twice.
