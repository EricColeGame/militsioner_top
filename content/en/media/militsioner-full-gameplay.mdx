## The Colossal Policeman: How the Watching System Works

Most stealth games give you a patrol route and a cone of vision. Militsioner gives you a giant with opinions. HE is always present, always watching, and HIS single job is to stop you from leaving town. You cannot outrun HIM, and brute force is a losing bet, so the entire game becomes about managing HIM rather than defeating HIM.

The design is explicitly kafkaesque, and the store description lays out the emotional toolkit: you can anger, flatter, disappoint, deceive, or even romance the Colossal Policeman. That is not flavor text — it is the mechanical core. Every interaction is a small deposit into a relationship that determines how tightly the town is locked down around you.

The second layer is memory. HE recalls what you did last time and adjusts his behavior accordingly. Repeat the same trick twice and you should expect a different, sharper response. That single feature is what turns a short demo loop into something with genuine replay value.

| Mood lever | How you pull it | What it buys you | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flattery | Compliments, gifts, small favors | A softer, more distracted watchman | Can read as manipulation if overused |
| Deception | Misdirection, false trails, lies | Space to move unseen | HE remembers the trick next attempt |
| Disappointment | Breaking promises, failing tasks | Unpredictable openings | Raises overall suspicion |
| Anger | Provocation, open defiance | Forces HIS attention elsewhere briefly | Escalation and harsher lockdowns |
| Romance | Flowers, flirtation, a date | A genuinely different escape path | Slow, resource-hungry, high commitment |

## Escape Routes, Tools, and Their Trade-offs

The town offers multiple ways to "resolve" your confinement, and the official description is refreshingly blunt about the options: steal, sell, trade, break in, sneak out. What matters for planning is that each route costs something different — money, time, reputation, or the goodwill of a specific townsperson.

Player experience with the demo suggests the smartest approach is to scout first and commit second. Walking the town without triggering suspicion teaches you which buildings are accessible, which characters hold useful items, and where the Colossal Policeman's attention drifts. Only then does picking a route stop being a coin flip.

| Route | What it requires | Relative risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sneaking out | Timing, patience, knowledge of sightlines | Low per attempt | Slowest path to a first escape |
| Theft | Access to a target building | Medium | Funds everything else, but stains your record |
| Selling and trading | Items found around town (flowers, for example) | Low | Clean income with no direct confrontation |
| Breaking in | A tool or a key, plus a distraction | High | High reward, high chance of an early capture |
| Train conspiracy | The train driver's cooperation | Very high | Can end with a wrecked train and an injured driver |
| Employment | Taking a job at the train station | Low, long-term | Keeps the driver intact and opens new options |
| Seduction | Flowers kept as gifts, sustained attention | Medium | Opens a route most players never try |

The train driver is the clearest example of how consequences work. If you conspire with him to sneak aboard and the plan collapses, the train is smashed and he is hurt. Take the job at the station instead, and he stays fine. The town remembers, and so does HE.

## Planning a Militsioner Full Gameplay Run, Step by Step

A productive run is not a speedrun. It is a sequence of small decisions that keep your options open as long as possible. The table below is a practical order of operations that works whether you are chasing your first escape or your tenth.

| Phase | Goal | Practical tip |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scout | Map the town and note who is where | Do not steal anything yet; learn the layout first |
| 2. Build income | Get money without drawing heat | Collect and sell flowers or trade for items |
| 3. Test the mood system | Learn how HE reacts to each lever | Try flattery before provocation — it is cheaper |
| 4. Pick a primary route | Commit to one escape plan | Keep a backup: theft if seduction stalls, sneaking if theft fails |
| 5. Fail deliberately | Learn the capture state | A capture is data, not a dead end |
| 6. Iterate | Change one variable per attempt | New approach, new response from the Colossal Policeman |

Two habits separate smooth runs from frustrating ones. First, stay quiet — the store page's advice to "get creative but stay quiet" is genuine mechanical guidance, not marketing copy. Second, do not spend a rare item early. Flowers, for instance, are worth more as a romantic bargaining chip than as quick cash, depending on which ending you are chasing.
