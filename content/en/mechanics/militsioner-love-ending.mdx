The giant policeman has been chasing you through flooded streets for hours, so the idea that he could be the one you walk away with sounds absurd — yet the Militsioner love ending is real, and it turns the game's central chase into a strange, slow-burn courtship. Why does it matter? Because the romance route changes almost everything about how a run plays out: which upgrades you spend time on, how you read the giant's moods, and what the final trial actually means. Community reports suggest the Militsioner love ending isn't a hidden cutscene you stumble into — it's a system you build toward, one emotion at a time.

## What the Militsioner Love Ending Actually Is

Militsioner is a surreal escape sim about being small. You play a citizen in a town watched over — and occasionally crushed — by an enormous policeman who can grab you, block your path, and throw you into a cell. For most of the game he is the obstacle. The Militsioner love ending inverts that relationship: instead of slipping past him, you get close to him.

It isn't a single dialogue pick, either. Based on community reports and wiki documentation, the romance is a track you build across a whole run — mood management, gifts, flirtation, and finally a date invitation — and it pays off at the game's trial rather than at the town gates.

| Element | Standard run | Romance route (community reports) |
|---|---|---|
| The giant's role | Obstacle and threat | Character with a mood you manage |
| Giant upgrade screen | Escape and movement tools | Adds a dedicated love option |
| Item usage | Keys, tools, distractions | Flowers given as gifts |
| Pacing | Move fast, avoid attention | Slow down, build trust over days |
| Final trial | Escape or capture outcome | A personal, relationship-driven conclusion |

Thematically, this fits what the developers have said about him. In an interview with *Rock Paper Shotgun* about the game's giant ambition, they explain why he doesn't simply escape and why he sits on the ground creating a deep pit that slowly floods — the point is that he is a character with reasons, not a mindless monster. The romance is the emotional version of that same idea.

## How the Emotion System Gates the Romance

Before you can flirt, you have to make him happy. That single line hides most of the route's complexity, because the giant's emotional state changes constantly and each state accepts different interactions.

Player experience is fairly consistent here: gifts only land when they match what he is currently thinking about. Hand him a flower while he is suspicious or angry and you lose the item for nothing. Wait until the thought appears above his current emotion, and the same flower does real work.

| Mood state | How it reads in play | What works | What wastes your turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calm / neutral | Slow movement, low aggression | Observe, stay nearby, small interactions | Flirting too early |
| Curious | Attention fixed on an object or thought | Give the matching gift | Ignoring the window |
| Happy | Open, relaxed body language | Flirting, then the date dialogue | Over-gifting past the threshold |
| Angry / suspicious | Aggressive pursuit, grabbing | Back off and wait it out | Any gift or flirt attempt |

Think of it as a gate rather than a meter. You are not grinding affection points; you are waiting for a specific opening and then acting inside it. Miss the opening and nothing breaks — you simply try again later. Force it and you can stall the whole route for a while.

## Step-by-Step: Unlocking the Militsioner Love Ending

The order below reflects the sequence reported by the community. Treat it as a checklist rather than a speedrun script, because the giant's mood is never fully predictable.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play normally until the relationship track opens | The route needs context before it unlocks |
| 2 | Get jailed or drowned | Community reports say the giant upgrade screen opens in these states |
| 3 | Enable the love option on that upgrade screen | This is the flag that starts the romance route |
| 4 | Keep him calm and build his mood | Gifts only count in the right emotional state |
| 5 | Give him three flowers | Three is the threshold repeatedly cited by players |
| 6 | Flirt once he is happy enough | Opens the date dialogue branch |
| 7 | Ask him out on a date | The turning point of the route |
| 8 | Ask him to leave with you | Leads toward the ending itself |

Two steps trip people up more than any others. The first is step 2 — many players never see the love option because they never end up in the jailed or drowned state where the upgrade screen appears. The second is step 5, where players dump every flower they own at once instead of spacing them across mood windows.

If you are following a written guide and nothing is happening, slow down and check the giant's current emotion before assuming the route is broken. Most reported "bugs" are just mistimed gifts.

## Flowers, Timing, and the Gift Economy

Flowers are the only romance item named consistently across community reports, and their value is almost entirely about timing rather than quantity. One flower given at the right moment is worth three given at the wrong one.

| Timing window | Signal to watch for | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Early run, low mood | He is idle or neutral | Minimal mood gain |
| Matching thought | The item appears above his current emotion | Full mood gain |
| While angry | Aggressive movement, grabbing | Gift wasted, mood drops |
| After three flowers | He stays happy across interactions | Flirt and date options open |
| During the date | Dialogue branch active | Route progresses toward the ending |

A useful habit is to hold one flower in reserve at all times. Because mood shifts fast, having a gift ready the instant a matching thought appears is the difference between a smooth route and an hour of waiting.

It also helps to think about what the flowers represent. You are not buying affection with items — you are proving attention. The giant notices that you were paying enough attention to bring the right thing at the right time, and that is what the system is actually measuring.

### What the ending itself looks like

The romance route resolves around the trial, the moment the game has been building toward the entire time. The town has grown large enough to burst out into the world, and he is already eager to meet you in person — which is exactly the tension the love route leans into. Instead of a chase, you get a confrontation that is personal.

Community reports describe the conclusion as quieter than the escape endings: less about outrunning him and more about what the two of you decide. That tonal shift is the whole point. You spent the run learning his moods, his timing, and his reasons, and the ending pays that attention back.

## FAQ

**Is the Militsioner love ending hard to get?**
It is less difficult than it is easy to miss. The route depends on reaching the jailed or drowned state so the love option appears on the giant upgrade screen, then managing his mood carefully. Players who rush usually never see the option at all.

**Do I need exactly three flowers for the Militsioner love ending?**
Three is the number community reports cite most often as the threshold before flirting works reliably. The more important rule is timing: give each flower when it matches what he is currently thinking about, not on a fixed schedule.

**Can I still escape if I start the romance route?**
Yes. Enabling the love option does not lock you out of movement or escape tools, so you can keep playing normally while building the relationship. Just be aware that gifts and mood management take time you would otherwise spend escaping.

**What happens if I flirt too early?**
Nothing permanent. The attempt simply fails and you lose that window, so raise his mood again with a well-timed gift and retry. The route is forgiving about order, but it is strict about timing.
