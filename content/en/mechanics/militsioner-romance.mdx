Every escape route in Militsioner runs through the same towering obstacle: the Colossal Policeman who tracks your every move. Most players try to anger, flatter, or deceive him, but the **Militsioner romance** path lets you win him over instead, and it may be the strangest and most satisfying way out of town. If you have heard whispers about dating the giant and want to know whether it is real, this guide walks through the full **Militsioner romance** mechanic, from unlocking the love option to landing the date dialogue.

## Why the Romance Route Exists at All

Militsioner is built on emotional manipulation. The game's [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1373530/Militsioner/) describes a loop where you can anger, flatter, disappoint, deceive, or even romance the giant who follows you. Romance is not a hidden joke — it is one of several designed levers you can pull to change how the Colossal Policeman treats you.

That matters because the giant is both your jailer and your audience. Every action you take is observed, so the emotional state you cultivate determines which doors open. Players who treat the policeman purely as an enemy often hit walls; players who treat him as a relationship to manage find new dialogue, new outcomes, and a very different tone.

### The Five Emotional Approaches

| Approach | What You Do | Risk Level | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anger | Provoke him into losing his composure | High | Distraction plays and chaotic runs |
| Flattery | Compliment him to soften his guard | Low | Buying early breathing room |
| Disappointment | Let him down to shift his attention | Medium | Stalling while you reposition |
| Deception | Lie and misdirect him | Medium–High | Players who enjoy high-risk routes |
| Romance | Build affection until he willingly lets you go | Low–Medium | Story-focused players and completionists |

Each route has its own dialogue tree, and the romance branch is the only one where the giant's approval of you becomes the goal rather than a side effect.

## How to Unlock the Love Option

According to community reports compiled on the Militsioner Wiki's Solutions page, the romance path does not appear on a normal playthrough right away. You first need to end up in a failure state — specifically, being jailed or drowned — because that is what brings up the giant upgrade screen.

The love option lives inside that upgrade screen. Enable it there, and the romance dialogue becomes available once you are back in the world. This is a genuinely important detail: a lot of players never see the Militsioner romance content because they avoid getting caught at all costs. In this game, getting caught can be progress.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Get jailed or drowned | Triggers the giant upgrade screen |
| 2 | Open the upgrade screen | This is where romance is offered |
| 3 | Enable the love option | Turns on the romance dialogue tree |
| 4 | Resume normal play | You can now build the relationship |
| 5 | Keep his mood positive | Romance only advances when he is happy |

If you have already passed a jail or drowning sequence and skipped the screen, check whether the option is still available the next time you are caught.

## Raising His Mood: The Happiness Loop

Romance in Militsioner is not a single dialogue choice — it is a mood-management loop. The giant has a visible emotional state, and the way you interact with him either nudges it upward or drags it down. Community reports consistently describe one core rule: gift him something when the thought bubble above his head matches the item you are about to give.

Timing is everything here. Handing over a flower while he is thinking about something else is often wasted, whereas the same flower given at the right moment produces a noticeable jump in mood. Once he is happy enough, flirt options open up, and eventually the date dialogue becomes available.

| What You See | What It Means | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| Thought bubble matches an item you carry | He is receptive right now | Gift it immediately |
| Thought bubble shows something else | His attention is elsewhere | Wait, do not waste the item |
| Neutral expression, no bubble | Mood is stable | Use small positive interactions |
| Visible irritation or anger | Mood has dropped | Back off and avoid provoking him |
| Clearly happy, upbeat reactions | You are near the flirt threshold | Try flirting, then the date option |

### Practical Mood Tips

- Watch the thought bubble before every gift, not after.
- Do not spam interactions. Player experience suggests the giant responds to well-timed gestures more than volume.
- Avoid mixing routes. Angering him after building affection usually undoes progress.
- Treat each flower as a limited resource and save them for moments when his thought bubble agrees with you.

## Three Flowers and the Date Dialogue

The community-documented sequence is refreshingly simple on paper: make him happy, give him three flowers, and then ask him out on a date. The difficulty is entirely in the execution, because each flower has to land during a receptive moment.

| Milestone | Requirement | What Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Love option enabled | Reach the giant upgrade screen via jail or drowning | Romance dialogue tree |
| Flower one | Gift while his thought bubble matches | First mood jump |
| Flower two | Sustain happiness, then gift again | Deeper affection responses |
| Flower three | Third successful, well-timed gift | Flirt options appear |
| Flirt | Happiness threshold reached | Relationship advances further |
| Ask him out | After flirting successfully | Date dialogue and its outcomes |

Once the date dialogue triggers, you are in the endgame of the romance branch. This is where the Militsioner romance route pays off narratively — the conversation shifts from a jailer-and-prisoner dynamic to something much more unusual, and the escape you get is not the one you originally planned.

## How Romance Compares to the Other Routes

Players deciding where to spend their time should know that romance is not the fastest route. It is, however, one of the most forgiving once it is running, because a happy giant is a predictable giant.

| Route | Setup Cost | Ongoing Effort | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anger | Low | High — must keep provoking | Easy to start, hard to control |
| Flattery | Low | Low | Easy, but shallow rewards |
| Disappointment | Medium | Medium | Moderate |
| Deception | Low | High | Risky if caught |
| Romance | Medium — requires a jail or drowning | Medium — mood upkeep | Hard to undo once invested |

The trade-off is clear: romance asks for an upfront failure state and steady attention, then rewards you with a calmer, more cooperative giant and a distinctive storyline.

### Final Tips for a Smooth Romance Run

- Save before gifting if you are unsure about timing.
- Keep a small buffer of flowers rather than spending them the moment you find them.
- Stay consistent — the giant reacts to patterns, not one-off gestures.
- Do not rush the flirt step. Player reports suggest patience produces cleaner dialogue outcomes.

## FAQ

**Can you actually romance the Militsioner in the game?**
Yes. Romance is one of the emotional routes the game explicitly supports, and the [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1373530/Militsioner/) lists it alongside anger, flattery, disappointment, and deception. It requires unlocking the love option through the giant upgrade screen first.

**How many flowers do I need for the Militsioner romance path?**
Community reports point to three flowers as the threshold, followed by flirting and then the date dialogue. Each flower needs to be gifted when his thought bubble matches the item.

**What if the love option never shows up?**
The most common reason is that you have not been jailed or drowned yet. That failure state is what opens the giant upgrade screen where the romance toggle lives.

**Does the romance route change how you escape?**
It changes the tone and the path rather than removing the goal. Instead of tricking or outlasting the Colossal Policeman, you build enough affection that he willingly lets you go — a very different ending to the same story.
