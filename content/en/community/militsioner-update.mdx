Getting caught by a towering policeman was always the point of Militsioner — but the latest **Militsioner update** makes the chase fairer, stranger, and far more replayable. Developer Tallboys and publisher CRITICAL REFLEX laid out the changes in their June 2025 progress report, and the headline is simple: the giant now has moods, pockets, hobbies, and a day-off schedule you can actually plan around.

If you bounced off earlier builds because the sandbox felt thin, this Militsioner update gives every run a reason to keep going. Below is a full breakdown of what changed, what it means for your escape plan, and how to exploit the new systems before the Law leans in for a closer look.

## The Militsioner Update Overhauls the Star of the Show

The first thing the developers tackled was the Militsioner himself. His Mood System has been rebalanced, and his grab-and-catch tech is now smoother and more physically believable. According to the team, the goal was to make being caught feel like a consequence of your choices rather than a random spike of aggression.

He also gained a personal inventory. Items vanish into his giant pockets and come back out when he needs them — unless you lift them first. Theft is possible, but getting spotted mid-pickpocket lands in your case file, and a bad case file follows you into later attempts.

| System | Before (community reports) | After the update |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catch mechanic | Abrupt, sometimes unfair grabs | Smoother, more physically realistic |
| Mood System | Hard to read, swingy | Rebalanced and easier to influence |
| Inventory | None | Giant pockets you can steal from |
| Interaction range | Mostly passive patrolling | Leans in to inspect, question, and search |
| NPC behavior | Neighbors stayed quiet | Neighbors report break-ins to the police |

He can now lean down to study the town up close — inspecting crime scenes, questioning witnesses, or scanning the streets for you. When he does, noise becomes your enemy. Sprinting through a courtyard or smashing a window during one of these sweeps practically paints a target on your back.

The town's residents also got an upgrade, and not a friendly one. Break into an apartment where someone can see you, and they will call the police without hesitation. It's a small change with big ripple effects: stealth now depends on sightlines and timing, not just on staying out of the giant's shadow.

## Good Behavior Pays: Mood, Warnings, and Rooftop Rides

Playing clean — or at least never getting caught — opens a second path through the game. A happy Militsioner lets you walk the streets freely, and if you ask politely, he may even hand over money. Get caught red-handed and he can let you off with a warning instead of a game over.

The standout new perk is transport. He can lift you to the rooftops, cutting out a long and dangerous climb. That turns good standing into a genuine route option, not just a flavor bonus.

| Militsioner's Mood | Effect on the Town | Effect on You |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Happy | Water levels drop, opening previously blocked passages | Free movement, cash gifts, rooftop lifts, warnings instead of punishment |
| Neutral | Town operates normally | Standard patrols and suspicion |
| Unhappy | Lower districts flood gradually | Blocked paths, unreachable objectives, forced detours |

The flood system deserves special attention because it was rebuilt for this version. Water now rises gradually, creeping up from the lower districts and cutting off routes one by one. Some objectives become impossible until you figure out how to cheer the policeman up. The reverse is also true — a happy giant lowers the water further than before, exposing passages you could never reach on a normal day.

That creates an interesting tension. Do you spend a cycle keeping him content so you can reach a flooded basement, or do you let him stew and use the chaos as cover? Both are viable, and both change how the map reads.

## Rogue-Lite Progression and the Mind-Map

The developers are upfront that they don't expect anyone to escape on the first try. The Law's hands are long and its eyes never fully close. So the Militsioner update introduces a progression layer that borrows from rogue-lites without turning the game into a grind.

Everything you accomplish before your inevitable demise carries forward. Each failed attempt earns currency you can spend on upgrades, letting you nudge the environment and the giant's behavior to make life easier for your next character. Successful escapes unlock broader influence over the world.

| Progression Element | How You Earn It | What It Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upgrade currency | Unsuccessful attempts | Environmental and behavioral tweaks |
| Influence unlocks | Successful escapes | New things you can change before a run |
| World events | Time passing in-game | Doors opening, bridges getting repaired |
| Mind-Map entries | Discovering clues and opportunities | Tracked objectives and route planning |

The Mind-Map is the companion system here, and it's the feature most likely to save your run. Every lead you stumble onto — a ticket cashier who needs a drink, a VHS tape describing remote corners of town — gets logged automatically. You can return to a task whenever you're ready instead of juggling a mental checklist. It doubles as an in-game reference for mechanics, which is handy given how many interlocking systems now exist.

One caveat: the town does not wait for you. Days pass, unique events fire, and the environment shifts whether you're ready or not. Community reports suggest treating the Mind-Map as a menu of opportunities rather than a to-do list — the window on some leads closes faster than others.

## Days Off, Trains, and a Refurnished Town

Giants need rest too. Every four in-game days, the Militsioner takes a day off. He's noticeably happier, new travel options appear, and you get a rare stretch of breathing room. You can even spend part of it getting to know him — the report teases the possibility of a date with a giant, which could thaw the endless cat-and-mouse dynamic.

Or you can do the opposite and use his absence to run your shadier errands in peace. The developers deliberately leave that choice open, and it's one of the clearest examples of the immersive-sim philosophy behind the project.

The town itself changed in several meaningful ways:

| Town Change | Details | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two trains | A passenger train at midday, a cargo train in the evening | Two distinct escape windows with different tradeoffs |
| Vending machines | Coffee, croissants, mineral water, and more | Spreads supplies across the map and rewards exploration |
| Randomized cars | Vehicle contents reshuffle every run | Kills memorization, forces improvisation |
| New art assets | Down-to-earth but dream-like direction | Every object is meant to imply a story |

The car rework is the biggest shift for veteran players. Previously, each vehicle had a fixed loot table, so a route could be memorized and repeated. Now configurations change with every run, which means the players who thrive will be the ones who adapt rather than the ones who grind a script.

The two trains add a scheduling layer on top of that. Midday and evening arrivals give you two hard deadlines to plan around, and the differences between passenger and cargo routes mean the safer option isn't always the better one.

## Practical Tips for Your Next Escape Attempt

Putting all of this together, here's how to approach a fresh run after the Militsioner update:

- **Protect your case file.** Petty theft from the giant's pockets isn't worth a permanent record unless you have a guaranteed exit.
- **Go quiet when he leans in.** Those inspection sweeps are when most players get flagged. Stop moving, stop breaking things.
- **Keep an alibi handy.** Public unrest makes you the default suspect, and the town remembers.
- **Bank upgrades between runs.** Currency earned from failures is the single most reliable long-term investment.
- **Log everything in the Mind-Map.** A drink for a cashier or a VHS tape can be the key to a route you haven't found yet.
- **Time your escapes to the trains.** Midday and evening are your two structural windows.
- **Plan around the four-day cycle.** A day off is either a social opportunity or a free crime spree — decide before it starts.
- **Watch the water.** If the flood is rising, your priority shifts from escaping to cheering up a giant.

None of these tips require memorizing the map, which is clearly the intent. With randomized cars and shifting flood levels, preparation beats route knowledge every time.

## FAQ

**Is the Militsioner update available now?**
The progress report describes changes already in the latest build of the game, with more development details promised in future reports. Check the Steam page for the current patch state before you start a run.

**Can you really steal from the Militsioner?**
Yes. He keeps items in his giant pockets, and you can take them. If he catches you in the act, it goes on your record and hurts your standing with him.

**What happens if the Militsioner is unhappy?**
The lower parts of town flood gradually, blocking paths and making some objectives unreachable until you calm him down. When he's happy, the water recedes further than usual and opens new passages.

**Does progress carry over after you die?**
Yes. The progression system works like a rogue-lite: failed attempts earn currency for upgrades, and successful escapes unlock new ways to influence the environment and the giant's behavior on later runs.
