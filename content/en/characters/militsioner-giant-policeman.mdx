## Who Is the Militsioner Giant Policeman?

The character the game calls the Colossal Policeman is the heart of the entire premise. He is enormous, he is always somewhere on the horizon, and he is explicitly described as the one whose task is to stop you from escaping. You are not sneaking past a patrol route or a guard tower — you are sneaking past a person, one who can see you, judge you, and remember you.

What separates him from a typical monster is that he has feelings. The official description lists anger, flattery, disappointment, deception, and even romance as valid ways to interact with him. That single design choice changes everything: he is not a wall to climb, he is a relationship to manage.

## How the Colossal Policeman Watches and Reacts

The marketing line is blunt about it: every move you make, every step you take, he will be watching you. In practice, that means the game leans hard into stealth, quiet movement, and timing. The Steam tags back this up — atmospheric, stealth, first-person, immersive sim, exploration — and they tell you what kind of pressure to expect.

But surveillance is only half the system. The other half is his mood. According to the developers, you can anger him, flatter him, disappoint him, deceive him, or romance him, and those are not cosmetic dialogue options. They are tools with consequences.

| Approach | What It Seems to Do | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| Flattery | Softens his mood and buys you breathing room | Low, but wears thin |
| Deception | Redirects his attention while you move | Medium — collapses if caught |
| Disappointment | Lets him underestimate you | Low, but hard to recover from |
| Anger | Provokes a reaction you can exploit | High — raises scrutiny |
| Romance | Builds a personal bond, including the option of a date | High investment, unusual payoff |

Community discussion around the demo has focused heavily on that last option, because a game that lets you take a giant policeman on a date is doing something almost nobody else is attempting. Whether you lean into it or avoid it entirely is up to you — but ignoring his emotional state is rarely the smart play.

## Escaping the Town: Routes, Risks, and What He Remembers

Militsioner is built around nonlinear problem solving. The Steam description encourages players to steal, sell, trade, break in, and sneak out, and it frames these as legitimate paths rather than moral failings. What matters is that you find a way out, not that you find a clean one.

| Escape Route | How It Works | Trade-Off |
|---|---|---|
| Train stowaway | Conspire with the train driver to board secretly | Can fail violently if rushed |
| Legitimate employment | Take a job at the train station instead | Slower, but far safer for everyone |
| Theft and resale | Steal goods, sell them, fund your plan | Raises your profile in town |
| Trading and favors | Build goodwill and resources with residents | Requires patience and social reading |
| Breaking and entering | Access restricted buildings for tools or intel | High risk if he is watching |
| Pure stealth | Slip out quietly with no support network | Hardest to pull off, cleanest result |

The train example from the official description is worth studying closely. If you conspire with the driver and the plan falls apart, the train ends up smashed and the driver ends up injured. Take the slower route — get a job at the station instead — and the driver stays fine. The lesson is not that one path is correct. It is that the town remembers what you did.

| Your Action | Immediate Result | Long-Term Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rushed the train plan | Train destroyed, driver hurt | A worse town, and fewer allies |
| Worked at the station first | Slower escape prep | Driver unharmed, options preserved |
| Got caught last attempt | Escape failed | He adjusts his behavior next time |
| Tried a brand-new approach | Unknown outcome | He has less data to predict you |

That last row matters more than it looks. The developers have stated that the Colossal Policeman will recall what you did previously and change how he behaves because of it. Repeating the same trick twice is a losing strategy.

## Tips for Outsmarting the Militsioner Giant Policeman

1. **Scout before you commit.** Exploration is a core tag for a reason. Learn the town's layout, note where he tends to focus, and only then pick a plan.
2. **Stay quiet by default.** Noise is the cheapest way to lose. Move deliberately, use cover, and treat every open street as exposed ground.
3. **Bank resources early.** Flowers, goods, and money all convert into leverage. Selling what you find funds the tools your escape actually needs.
4. **Rotate your strategy.** Because he adapts to your last attempt, a fresh approach is often stronger than a perfected one.
5. **Treat his mood as a resource.** Flattery, honesty, and even romance are legitimate mechanics, not jokes. A calm policeman is a distracted policeman.
6. **Think about who else pays for your plan.** The train driver is the clearest example: your shortcut can become someone else's injury.
7. **Learn from every capture.** Getting caught is part of the loop. Note what he noticed, then remove that tell from your next run.

If you want a simple rule to follow, it is this: plan like a strategist, move like a thief, and talk like a diplomat. The game rewards players who switch between all three.
