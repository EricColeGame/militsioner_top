## What Is Militsioner?

Militsioner is a first-person immersive sim about being watched. You play an ordinary nobody — no heroic resume, no grand destiny — who is suddenly arrested for a crime you may or may not have committed. Your passport and a half-eaten banana are apparently your best assets. The town around you is surreal, hostile, and full of people with strong opinions about your guilt.

Hovering over all of it is the Colossal Policeman, a building-sized officer whose entire job is to keep you from leaving. He watches. He remembers. He gets moody. Your job is to get out anyway — through stealth, theft, trade, trickery, or something stranger.

## Gameplay Deep Dive: Outsmarting the Colossal Policeman

The pitch is simple to state and complicated to execute: multiple routes to freedom, and a giant cop who adapts to what you did last time. The store description leans hard on verbs — anger him, flatter him, disappoint him, deceive him, or romance him.

| Escape Approach | What It Costs You | Risk Level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pure stealth | Time, patience, route knowledge | Medium |
| Theft and breaking in | Reputation with townsfolk | High |
| Trading and selling goods | Inventory you might need later | Low |
| Conspiring with NPCs | Their safety if the plan fails | High |
| Flirting / dating the Policeman | Your dignity, possibly your freedom | Unpredictable |
| Brute force | Almost everything | Very high |

Mood management is the mechanic that ties it together. The Colossal Policeman isn't a static hazard — he has states, and your actions move him between them. Bring him flowers and he might soften. Sell those same flowers for cash and you might need a different plan entirely.

Consequences are persistent, and the store page gives a concrete example: conspire with the train driver to sneak aboard, and the plan can collapse, wrecking the train and injuring him. Take a job at the station instead, and the driver stays fine. The town remembers what you did, and so does the Policeman — he adjusts his behavior based on your previous attempt.

That memory system is what makes replayability structural rather than cosmetic. Getting caught isn't a fail state so much as a data point. Each failed escape teaches the AI something, and your next run starts from a slightly different relationship with the law.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of the Militsioner Demo

The demo rewards experimentation over efficiency. If you play it like a conventional stealth game and restart every time you're spotted, you'll miss the point.

- **Get caught on purpose once.** Watching how the Colossal Policeman reacts to a capture teaches you more about his behavior model than an hour of successful sneaking.
- **Talk to everyone before you steal anything.** Reputation appears to be a resource. Once you burn a relationship, you may not get it back in the same run.
- **Test the mood system early.** Give a gift, then insult the same character and observe the shift. Understanding how quickly moods move tells you how much margin you have.
- **Scout before you commit.** The town is built for parkour and vertical routes. Knowing three exits from a district beats knowing one perfect path.
- **Keep items instead of selling them.** Flowers and similar items serve dual purposes — currency and relationship currency. Selling early can lock you out of a romance or bribe route.
- **Read the announcement feed.** The Militsioner Steam page posts events and news updates, and the demo's scope has expanded since the original reveal.
- **Treat failure as progress.** The Policeman recalls your last attempt and changes his behavior. A "bad" run still improves your next one.

One more practical note: with English as the only fully voiced language, players who prefer subtitles should confirm the subtitle size options in the settings menu before starting. The town is atmospheric and dark by design, and reading subtitles over a moody backdrop is a real consideration.
